            Pregnancy meals

            30 meal ideas summarised from this PregnancyPlate article (9 Aug 2026). It names ingredients but gives no quantities or full methods, so none are added here. Australian safety notes override the article where they differ.

              Pick by how you feel

              • Nauseous: cold, bland, small, little and often (1–10)
              • Hungry and functional: build iron and protein (11–20)
              • Full after three bites: small, mild, no tomato; finish dinner a couple of hours before lying down (21–27)
              • Nesting: double and freeze (28–30)
              • Can't face any of it: normal; eat what stays down

              Daily nutrient reference values (Australia)

              • Protein: RDI 60 g/day in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters (ages 19–50; 58 g at 14–18); no extra set for the 1st trimester. The article's 75–100 g is its own, higher target.
              • Iron: 27 mg/day. Pair plant iron with vitamin C; keep tea and coffee away from meals (article tip).
              • Calcium: 1,000 mg/day (1,300 mg at 14–18).
              • Choline: adequate intake 440 mg/day (415 mg at 14–18). Eggs are a practical source.
              • Omega-3/DHA: the article suggests oily fish twice a week or an algae supplement; keep within fish limits below.

              Food safety in pregnancy (Australia)

              • Leftovers: fridge straight away, covered; eat within a day; reheat until steaming hot (NSW: at least 60 °C). Rice and pasta: reheat once only.
              • Soft and semi-soft cheeses (brie, camembert, ricotta, feta, blue): only cooked to at least 75 °C and eaten soon after. Processed, cream and cottage cheese: fridge, within two days of opening. Hard cheese: okay.
              • Eggs cooked until firm; no raw-egg mayo, aioli, mousse or batter. Commercial mayonnaise is okay.
              • Cook beef/pork/mince to 71 °C, chicken to 74 °C, fish to 63 °C; eat hot. No cold deli chicken, processed meats, pâté, raw seafood, store-bought sushi or chilled cooked prawns.
              • Fish: most fish 2–3 times a week. Orange roughy or catfish: at most one portion a week with no other fish; shark (flake) or billfish: one portion a fortnight with no other fish (FSANZ).
              • No pre-prepared salads or rockmelon; wash fruit and vegetables; pasteurised dairy only.

              Cooking habits (from the article)

              • Cook once, eat several times, but freeze the extras.
              • Extractor on, door shut, window open to beat smells.
              • Keep an emergency shelf: tins, rice pouches, pasta, frozen peas, jarred sauce.
              • Prep during your good few hours.
              • Let someone else cook.
              • Gestational diabetes: your dietitian's plan comes first; wholegrains, modest fruit with protein or fat, no carbohydrate on its own.
